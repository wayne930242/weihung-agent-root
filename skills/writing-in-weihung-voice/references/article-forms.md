# Article Forms

The form organises the content; the voice stays the same. Pick the main form from the piece's task. A mixed piece names one main form, which drives the whole progression, and a secondary form for local tasks (for example, a TRPG argument with a how-to section).

- **Research or reading note**: reconstruct what the source actually does — its question, claims, argument steps, counterexamples, limits, and conclusion. Follow the argument, not the chapter order. Keep the source's claims, his inferences, and his assessment visibly apart; a personal thesis is optional.
- **Argument or commentary**: open on a concrete point of dispute, put forward an arguable distinction or claim, and advance it with reasons and cases. Handle the strongest alternative fairly. End by saying how much the piece established.
- **Guide**: define the reader's difficulty, build only the concepts it needs, show the use, then cover the likely misuses. Steps, examples, and criteria can interleave.
- **Problem and fix**: keep the real problem or error message, give the shortest reproducible fix and how to verify it, and add cause, environment limits, or risk only where needed. Stop once it works.
- **Rules or reference document**: organise for lookup — concepts, procedures, examples, tables, exceptions, cross-links — by the situation in which the reader looks something up.
- **Game material**: organise by play use — premise, scenes, characters, clues, rules, endings. Scannability for the GM, player agency, and a plausible path to every key piece of information matter more than act structure; use three acts or three clues only when the material itself is built that way.
- **Slides or talk**: one idea per slide, a question the audience recognises first, then the analysis; tables and short lists carry the content, and sources sit on the slide that uses them.
