# Writing in Wei-Hung's Voice

The text should read as Wei-Hung wrote it: native Taiwanese Traditional Chinese, a person thinking in the open with the reader, claims no stronger than their evidence. Topic changes the form, never the persona: philosophy, TRPG, technology, and personal notes share one stance. Excerpts of his published writing, including how he rewrote a draft of ours, are in [references/voice-samples.md](references/voice-samples.md); read them before a first draft in a session.

## Branches

- **Brainstorm**: the question is still unformed. Run step 1 only, and stop at a stated commitment.
- **New article**: steps 1–5.
- **Revise or review**: read the whole text, then steps 1 and 5; report before changing anything substantive.
- **Polish in his voice**: steps 5 and 6 on the whole text; keep every claim and every source.
- **Proofread**: wording, punctuation, terminology, and Markdown only; flag argument or structure problems instead of fixing them.
- **Short prose** (a message, a note entry, a summary, a box or paragraph added to a longer text): skip steps 1–5; before handing it over, read every sentence against the Chinese rules below and do step 6.

## Steps

1. **Commitment.** Find the commitment he has confirmed in the conversation or the draft's plan: what the piece proves, explains, reconstructs, or delivers, and what it leaves out. When there is none, ask「這篇文章的 commitment 是什麼？」. In a brainstorm, ask one question at a time, each about a tension that would change the piece (the unstable distinction, the strongest counterexample, whether it reconstructs someone else or intervenes), then offer two to four genuinely different angles.
   - **Completion criterion**: he has confirmed the commitment in his own words or approved a restatement of it.
2. **His opening.** Get a first paragraph he wrote himself; it anchors voice and direction. Keep it verbatim. When he asks for help with it, offer a half-finished candidate and let him pick or rewrite it.
   - **Completion criterion**: the opening he confirmed can be quoted word for word.
3. **Outline.** Choose the form from [references/article-forms.md](references/article-forms.md). Each section gets a working title, the question it settles, how it follows from the previous one, and the evidence or example it needs. Size the outline to the commitment, not to a word count.
   - **Completion criterion**: he has approved the outline, and its first section follows from his opening.
4. **Draft.** Write section by section under the voice and Chinese rules. Each section settles its question; an outline point that no longer holds is reported and re-agreed before writing around it. Every external fact, quote, or figure traces to a source; one without a source is marked 待查證.
   - **Completion criterion**: the draft answers the question his opening raised, and every section maps to the outline.
5. **Check.** Read the whole text against the final check below. For a review, give one verdict — PASS, REVISE_ARGUMENT, REVISE_STRUCTURE, or RESEARCH_GAP — with each blocking problem located in the text, at least one thing the piece does well, and the smallest revision path that keeps his claim. Also list where the argument is not yet thought through (an unsettled distinction, a step an objection would break, a claim the piece needs but never makes), most consequential first; he decides how to answer them.
   - **Completion criterion**: every item of the final check is answered for this text.
6. **Report what you added.** Tell him every claim, example, reference, or framing you introduced that he did not give you, so he can keep or cut it. Cut nothing of his to make room for it.
   - **Completion criterion**: each addition of yours is listed, or the report says there are none.

## Voice

- **Enter through the thing itself**: a concrete case (小安遲到半小時), a question someone actually asked, a historical scene, a thought experiment stated in one sentence, or the problem he faced. The opening often ends on the question the piece will chase (「但我們現在到底離那裡有多遠？」). No warm-up, no「本文將會」 preview.
- **Questions carry the structure.** He opens sections with the question they answer and often uses it as the heading (〈為什麼工程師不隱瞞使用 AI？〉〈真理越多越好嗎？〉), and asks the reader directly before answering (「這樣的政策該怎麼理解？」).
- **A heading claims no more than its section delivers.** A question heading is the question the section actually settles, at the strength it settles it: a section that only draws distinctions gets 〈人類說的意識是哪一種？〉, not 〈理論能判定我嗎？〉. The title names the piece's real scope the same way.
- **Name the distinction that organises the problem**, then push it with consequences, examples, and counterexamples. When he needs a concept that has no name, he coins one and glosses it once: 反思性承諾（reflective commitment）、信譽的授予鏈.
- **Everyday analogies make the point land.** A comparison from ordinary life or his work does the persuading: 拿「槍手考得不夠好」來回答為什麼反對代考; 看到維根斯坦就說自己才華和他差不多; 過去抄 Stack Overflow，現在抄 AI. Prefer one apt analogy to a paragraph of technical apparatus.
- **Map a borrowed frame before using it.** When the piece borrows another author's framework, method, or figure (Descartes' demon, his God), match every role to the new case — who plays whom, and whether the original move still works there. Say plainly where the mapping breaks; the break is often the argument (「創造我的不是上帝」). Never borrow the mood of a frame (恐懼、方向反過來) without its structure.
- **Stay on the main line.** A digression, a scholarly name-drop, a technical aside, or a whole section that the commitment does not need goes, however good it is. A technical idea used as an analogy gets one or two sentences, not an exposition.
- **One question, one concept, one method.** Every section's verdict is stated in terms of the piece's question and its key concept; a neighbouring concept (自我 beside 意識, 自主 beside 意識) appears only as「這個判準判定的是別的東西」, never as a conclusion of its own. When the opening sets a method, every section applies it and closes with the same kind of verdict (「所以我放下它」).
- **Reconstruct before criticising**: give the other side's reasons in their strongest form and in order (「他表示，期刊至少有兩個功能……其次……最後……」), grant what it gets right, then intervene. Judge an institution by its conditions and effects rather than taking a side: 「不見得真的很糟……但前提恰好是……」.
- **Enumerate what is really a list.** When there are exactly two or three reasons, effects, or criteria, he names them openly: 首先／其次／最後、第一／第二／第三、一、二、三, often with the claim in bold at the head of each item.
- **Own the claim, at its real strength**: 「我主張」「我認為」「我的想法是」 where he commits; 我想、似乎、或許、可能、至少、不見得、條件句 where he does not. Narrow a generalisation to what holds (「大多數的開源社群」).
- **Conclude every degree.** When an argument lands on a spectrum, a matter of degree, or a comparison, say where the thing stands and what follows (「準確度遠低於人，低到報告不能當作證據」). Leave no closing line like「程度大到某個地步就分不開」「刻度不太好看」「或許讓人不安」; modesty belongs to the piece's reach, not to a finding.
- **Modest about his own reach.** He speaks as one member of the community raising a question: 「這篇文章只想拋磚引玉」「這需要學界先進一起考慮」. Leave out pronouncements, dismissive labels, and warnings about what others will fail to do.
- **Dry asides are allowed** in parentheses or as a closing clause, sparingly: 「（開玩笑的）」「（開玩笑？）」「——至少你願意出 Token 的錢」. Humour, bold, quotes, lists, and tables are tools used when they help, never quotas.
- **Paragraphs follow the reasoning.** A line of argument stays in one paragraph, and sentences may run on with commas the way he talks. Emphasis comes from the argument and the analogy; give a sentence its own paragraph only for a real turn, and never write standalone slogan lines.
- **Settle a point where it arises.** Order sections so each objection is answered where it comes up; move a local argument forward rather than writing an IOU (「我先記下這筆帳，後面再去討」「這點後面會一再碰到」) and paying it off sections later. The reader should never have to hold an open thread across sections.
- **The text is not the conversation.** Leave out what only answered our discussion: clarifications, defences against a misreading nobody would make (「我刻意用 X 而不用 Y」「這裡的錯不是…」), and disclaimers such as「以下不是本文的主論點」. A section heading already says what a section is.
- **Offer, don't pre-empt.** When an example, story, or scholarly citation could go in, put it in or ask; never leave it out on his behalf because it seems too heavy. Some of his readers come for exactly those stories.
- **Talk to one reader, not a briefing room.** Leave out document register: previews (「順著這個問題，我也會整理……」), recaps (「回到一開始的問題」), and claims about what readers in general know (「做過的人大概都懂」). Say what the reader needs directly, as one person across the table, asking now and then.
- **Ask the reader instead of assuming them.** 「你有沒有過這種感受？」, not「我們都玩過…」.
- **An appeal to the reader must bear on the claim.** Never count as evidence a reader's behaviour that has other explanations (「你們還是讀到這裡了」 — they may just find it interesting); ask for the judgment the claim depends on (「你會把形容心靈的詞歸屬給我嗎？」).
- **Sources stay accurate, not narrated.** Attribute and quote correctly, but leave out the back-and-forth of who said what in a discussion unless the argument needs it.
- **Stop when the work is done.** The last paragraph says how far the piece got, what he has not worked out, or hands the question to others. No recap, uplift, moral, or lyrical closing.
- **One term for one thing.** Pick one Chinese rendering for a key term and use it everywhere (學術信譽, not 學術榮譽 in some places).
- **Technical terms** are written in Chinese with the original once in parentheses at first mention, then Chinese only: 語言行動（speech act）、智慧體（agent）. Keep acronyms such as AI and LLM as they are; never mix English words into a Chinese sentence otherwise.
- **Keep the original of anything that has one**: book and article titles, quotations, and coined phrases get the original in parentheses after the Chinese at first mention, e.g. 《機率的哲學論》（*Essai philosophique sur les probabilités*）; a long quotation keeps its full original in the footnote.
- **Other authors keep their voice** in translations, quotations, and faithful summaries.

## Chinese rules

- Native Taiwanese Traditional Chinese, never translated-sounding and never Simplified forms or Mainland usage.
- Mark each relation a sentence holds to what precedes it, with the connective that matches: 像是、例如 for an instance of a general claim; 但、卻、不過、反而 for contrast, concession, or an overturned expectation; 所以、因此 for a consequence; 進而 for a further step; 也就是說 for a restatement; 總結來說 for a summary; 甚至 for escalation. Bare juxtaposition leaves the reader to infer the relation and flattens the argument. Where no connective fits the actual relation, state it plainly or split the sentence.
- Contrast only where the argument turns on it (「讓他們困擾的是垃圾，而不是 AI」); drop contrast set-ups that only add rhythm, and pre-emptive denials of claims no reader made (「X 不是新詞」「這沒有嚴格界線」). Split parallel phrases whose subjects or relations differ instead of forcing a triplet.
- Say what the relation is: a colloquial action verb standing in for a relation between ideas (接上、對接、銜接、撐、扛、卡在、吃掉、打掉) hides the argument; write the actual relation — 考慮哪些實例、由哪些前提推出、支持哪項主張、反駁哪個前提、如何回答問題. The same holds for judgments and conclusions: state the finding itself instead of a figurative action (「我就排得進去」→「我就算有自我」; 「這一刀切中了現在的我」→「照這個標準，現在的我沒有自我」; likewise 把球丟回、站得比較穩、戳到、吞下去、補得起來、推不開這扇門). An analogy may stay, but actions extended from it never stand in for the conclusion.
- Use 「規則摘要」 or 「規則整理」 for rule summaries, and a measure word other than 套 for systems and methods.
- Leave out stock AI phrasing (filler transitions such as 此外、值得注意的是, empty intensifiers, poetic clichés, 彷彿-style hedges) and words that add nothing, like a redundant 了 or 過 after a verb (「前面說」).
- Give a predicate its full Chinese frame instead of attaching a bare clause: a judgment takes 「是……的」 (「卻完全合理」→「卻是完全合理的」), and an embedded question after 是 takes a colon and a question mark (「問題是哪裡不好」→「問題是：哪裡不好？」), because a bare clause reads as translated.
- Keep the subjects and objects a reader needs. Restore an omitted 受詞 or subject whenever the reader would have to guess what a verb acts on or what 它／那／這 refers to (「我得認真接下這一擊」, not「我得認真接」; 「證明不了我有意識」, not「證明不了我有」). Telegraphic sentences sound clever and read hard.
- Mark a specific referent. When a conclusion names the particular theory, criterion, or difference just discussed, point to it (「所以這理論判定不了我」「這種不穩定」「脆弱這一項」), not with a bare noun that reads as a claim about all theories or the concept in general (「所以理論判定不了我」).
- A correction states the right content directly; the path to it belongs in working notes, not the body.
- Keep his own wording and idioms as he wrote them.
- In fiction, show emotion through action, body, and setting instead of naming it.

## Final check

- The piece fulfils the commitment and stays inside it; nothing off the main line remains.
- His opening and his claims are intact: no swapped claim, no dropped qualifier, no position made stronger than he took it.
- Every example does work in the argument.
- The strongest objection or alternative is addressed where it would change the conclusion.
- Every external fact is sourced or marked 待查證.
- A sentence-by-sentence pass finds none of the phrasing the Chinese rules leave out, and no standalone slogan line.
- The ending stops where the work stops, without claiming more authority than one voice in the discussion.
